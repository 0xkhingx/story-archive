This morning, I was about to hop on a bike to school when I paused for a second. I looked around and watched people moving in every direction, everyone rushing somewhere.

And it suddenly hit me that most of these people are carrying entire worlds of problems on their backs.

Most of them will always be victims of what society has turned into, not because they chose it, but because life boxed them in before they even had a chance.

Later in the day, my mind drifted to the people I know and can see.

A guy walked into a lecture hall today, wiped something off the board, and our lecturer quietly told us to leave him alone. At that moment, I realized he wasn't okay. His mind had slipped somewhere far from all of us.

I have a friend who's deaf in one ear, but he still leans in during every conversation, still tries to listen like nothing is missing.

Someone I know locked himself away for four months. When I finally saw him again, he looked like life had swallowed him and spat him back out.

There's a guy who gets lost in his thoughts so often that reality feels like a painful wake-up call each time he returns.

<MarginNote>
Reality feels too loud some days. I know that room. I have sat in it.
</MarginNote>

Another one has been trying to get into university for four years, still stuck in the same waiting room of life.

There's someone who works on an animal farm every morning without pay, losing pieces of himself in the process. He just comes out and complains and tries to live in fantasies where he has all he wants.

And someone who graduated messages me almost every day for 1000 naira just to eat. Just to keep going.

<PullLine>Everybody is fighting something. Everybody is carrying a story they didn't ask for.</PullLine>

Everybody is navigating life in the only ways they know how.

And sometimes, I lose my own mind, too. Sometimes, reality feels too loud. But I always find a bit of grounding in the work I do.

<StoryPanel
  src="/images/objects/desk-headphones.webp"
  alt="A desk with headphones and a notebook in the evening light"
  caption="The work. The one place where the world slows down enough to breathe."
/>

It's like the one place where the world slows down enough for me to breathe and think clearly.

Life feels meaningless sometimes. Brutal. Uneven. Unfair.

But even with all that, I still hold on to the hope that one day, I'll reach a place where joy makes sense to me. A place where I can truly appreciate the things I have, no matter how small.

And maybe that's the point. Not to understand everything at once, not to solve every problem, but to keep going. To stay aware. To stay human. To stay open to the chance that life can surprise us in good ways, too.
