There were subtle messages everywhere that nobody was really paying attention to.

Aang and the group eventually decided to take matters into their own hands and find Appa by putting up fliers around Ba Sing Se.

Almost like a stroke of destiny, Jet found one. But guess who else did? Zuko.

And that had me thinking. Maybe Aang isn't the only main character in Avatar. Not main character-main character. Obviously Aang is the Avatar. But Zuko feels every bit as essential to the story as Aang is.

They're almost opposites, but not really. More like the same person in different realities.

Aang lost his people and his time and woke up not really knowing where to go. Then he found a family in Katara and Sokka and somehow still kept that happiness in him.

Zuko lost his family too, just in a completely different way. But Iroh was there for him. And even though Iroh clearly had a bigger goal of helping Zuko see himself differently and understand his own destiny, he never rushed the process or tried to convince Zuko that his thoughts weren't valid. He let him get there.

Jet had been brainwashed to the point where he didn't even remember some of his own people anymore. But his trauma was what triggered his memories to start coming back.

And that points to something I keep thinking about: maybe we can't completely avoid those triggers, no matter how much we try to push them back. Memories of love, joy, pain and suffering are permanently ingrained in us. They shape us. And no matter how much we try to run from the things that happened to us, I don't think there's really an escape from the way they become part of who we are.

Zuko, on the other hand, put the mask back on. The Blue Spirit. The enemy of the Fire Nation. He knew the Dai Li would probably know where the Avatar's bison was, so he went after one of their agents.

And Zuko was honestly a very intelligent and resourceful person. I feel like that gets overlooked sometimes. I genuinely think he could have been a better strategic asset to the Fire Nation than Azula.

But intelligence wasn't really Zuko's problem. Thinking things through was. And that's the part Iroh called him out on. Zuko had this habit of jumping headfirst into things without thinking about what came after. It was basically a staple of why Aang kept beating him.

Iroh even brought up the North Pole. Zuko had actually managed to capture Aang, but then what? He had nowhere to go. He got the thing he had spent all that time chasing and hadn't thought beyond getting it.

Then Zuko said he knew his own destiny. And Iroh asked him: “Your destiny? Or the destiny someone has tried to force on you?”

  src="/images/stories/blue-spirit/destiny-poster.webp"

Then he told him to look inward and start asking himself the big questions: Who are you? And what do you want?

<PullLine effect="quote">Your destiny? Or the destiny someone has tried to force on you?</PullLine>

That was the part that hit me the most. Because Zuko had spent so much time talking about his destiny, his honour, going home, his father — but how much of that was actually his?

Aang eventually found Appa and won the day. Zuko had to let go of the mask and start telling himself the truth. And only through Iroh's guidance did he even get to that point.

Then there's Jet. His whole goal had been to free his home from the Fire Nation and disrupt Fire Nation operations wherever he found them. But eventually I think he realized the Freedom Fighters were the whole thing for him. They saved him. They were his people. And they were there with him at the end.

His story was grief-filled, but it felt complete. He earned his redemption.
